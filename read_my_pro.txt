Absolutely. At this point, I would define the internal hackathon MVP as one complete, 
demonstrable journey rather than a collection of disconnected AI features. 
Smart Grievance Resolution Automation 
— MVP 
Core idea 
A multi-agent AI system that takes a citizen grievance from submission to 
verified resolution, while automatically planning actions, monitoring 
progress, checking evidence, escalating delays, and detecting recurrence. 
The important distinction is: 
We are NOT building another complaint-classification system. 
We are building the intelligent resolution layer after a grievance enters the system.

2. Step 1 — Citizen submits grievance 
The citizen opens the web portal. 
They see something simple: 
"Tell us what happened." 
Example: 
"There has been no water supply in Ward 5 for four days. Many houses are 
affected." 
They can optionally provide: 
● Location 
● Photo 
● Video 
● Document 
● Contact information 
For the MVP, text + image + location is enough. 
 
3. Step 2 — Triage Agent 
Agent: TriageAgent 
Model: GPT-OSS 120B 
The raw complaint goes to the first AI agent. 
It converts unstructured language into structured information. 
For example: 
{ 
} 
"summary": "Water supply unavailable in Ward 5 for four days", 
"department": "Water Supply", 
"category": "Water disruption", 
"location": "Ward 5", 
"priority": "HIGH", 
"affected_population": "Multiple households", 
"urgency_reason": [ 
"Essential service disruption", 
"Multiple citizens affected", 
"Four-day duration" 
] 
The important part is that the AI doesn't directly change the database. 
It produces structured output. 
Your backend validates and stores it. 
4. Step 3 — Resolution Planner Agent 
Agent: ResolutionPlannerAgent 
Model: GPT-OSS 120B 
This is where your project starts becoming different from a normal AI chatbot. 
The agent asks: 
"What actually needs to happen to resolve this?" 
It generates an executable action plan. 
Example: 
GRIEVANCE: GRV-1024 
 
1. Verify complaint location 
   Responsible: Ward Officer 
   Deadline: 4 hours 
 
2. Inspect water pipeline 
   Responsible: Field Team 
   Deadline: 12 hours 
 
3. Identify cause 
   Responsible: Water Department 
   Dependency: Inspection 
 
4. Repair fault 
   Responsible: Maintenance Team 
   Dependency: Cause identified 
 
5. Upload resolution evidence 
   Required: 
   - Before image 
   - After image 
   - Location 
   - Timestamp 
 
6. Citizen verification 
 
Now the complaint is no longer just: 
"Pending." 
It has become a workflow.

13. If citizen says NO 
This is one of the most important parts of the MVP. 
The citizen says: 
"Water supply is still unavailable." 
The system does not create a completely new complaint. 
Instead: 
Citizen rejects resolution 
          ↓ 
Existing case REOPENED 
          ↓ 
Officer notified 
          ↓ 
Supervisor notified 
          ↓ 
New action generated 
          ↓ 
Escalation if necessary 
 
This directly tackles the cycle: 
Complaint → fake/insufficient closure → citizen files again → duplicate.

Agent: RecurrenceAgent

This doesn't need to be an LLM-heavy operation. 
Use: 
Embeddings + vector similarity + location + time

Example: 
GRV-1024 
No water Ward 5 
 
GRV-1041 
Water pressure low Ward 5 
GRV-1088 
No water since Monday Ward 5 
GRV-1121 
Pipeline problem Ward 5 
System detects: 
Potential recurring issue

20. What is actually "Smart Automation" 
here? 
This is the most important thing for your presentation. 
You're automating five things: 
Understand 
AI converts messy human language into structured information. 
Plan 
AI creates the required resolution workflow. 
Monitor 
The system watches deadlines, dependencies and actions. 
Intervene 
The system automatically reminds and escalates when things get stuck. 
Verify 
AI checks evidence and citizen feedback before closure. 
And then: 
Learn 
The system detects recurring problems and converts individual complaints into systemic alerts. 
So the final concept becomes: 
Understand → Plan → Act → Monitor → Intervene → Verify → Learn 
That is the complete MVP. 
And for the internal hackathon, I would prioritize getting this one lifecycle working end-to-end 
over adding multilingual voice, WhatsApp, fancy maps, 20 analytics charts, or other peripheral 
features. If the judges can submit one complaint and watch the system intelligently carry it all 
the way to verified resolution + systemic insight, they'll immediately understand what makes 
the project different.
